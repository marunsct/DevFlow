## 1. Product name
Acme Scheduling Assistant

## 2. Users & stakeholders
| Who | Role | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Patients of small clinics | Primary end users who book appointments online | Evidence (brief) |
| Front desk staff | Stakeholders; currently field booking phone calls | Evidence (brief, implied) |
| Clinic owners/administrators | Stakeholders; need visibility of schedule and appointments | Assumption |

## 3. Problems, pain points & desired outcomes
- Problem (evidence): Small clinics currently depend on phone calls to the front desk for appointment booking, which is inconvenient for patients and burdens front-desk staff.
- Desired outcome (evidence): Patients can easily book appointments online instead of calling.
- Desired outcome (assumption): Reduced front-desk phone volume and a simple, low-maintenance tool appropriate for small clinics.

## 4. Core workflows (MVP-relevant)
1. Patient online booking (evidence of need): choose provider/service → pick an available date/time → confirm → receive confirmation. (Step detail is assumed.)
2. Clinic schedule/availability management (assumed): front desk or owner defines working hours, provider availability, and views booked appointments.
3. Cancellation / rescheduling (assumed): patient or staff changes an appointment within policy.

## 5. MVP candidates
- Single-clinic, web-based booking page (no login for patients).
- Provider/service selection + availability calendar driven by staff-defined hours.
- Appointment record (patient name, contact, provider, service, date/time).
- On-screen (and optionally email) booking confirmation.
- Simple staff view of upcoming appointments.

## 6. Deferred scope (assumed, not stated in brief)
- Multi-clinic/multi-tenant operation; EHR/calendar system integration.
- Payments, deposits, no-show fees; SMS/email reminders.
- Waitlists, recurring appointments, patient accounts/authentication, mobile app, analytics dashboards.

## 7. Constraints
- Small-clinic context implies limited IT resources and the need for simplicity (assumption from "small clinics").
- Workflow constraints (evidence): artifacts only under `docs/generated`; approval required; no command execution.
- Repository contains no existing booking/scheduling code to build upon (evidence: no app source found).

## 8. Evidence vs. assumptions
- Evidence: product name, target customers (small clinics), end users (patients), core need (online booking instead of phone), workflow scaffold and artifact/approval config.
- Assumptions: providers/services model, confirmation channels, cancellation policy, staff tooling, integrations, payments, success metrics, tech stack.

## 9. Success measures (proposed, to validate)
- Share of appointments booked online vs. by phone.
- Patient booking completion rate and time-to-book.
- Reduction in front-desk booking call volume.
- Show rate / cancellation rate; patient satisfaction.

2. Single clinic or multi-tenant SaaS? Who operates it?
3. Must it integrate with an existing calendar/EHR, or stand alone for MVP?
4. Who manages availability and appointment changes — front desk, owners, or patients?
5. What confirmation/notification channels are required (email, SMS), and what patient data is needed?
6. Are patient cancellation/rescheduling and policies (e.g., 24h notice) in scope?
7. Any regulatory/compliance requirements (e.g., health-data handling)?
8. Target deployment/hosting and required tech stack?
9. Budget/timeline and definition of "easy" (accessibility, mobile-responsive web?)
